# Stage 5 — Build Products & Price Them

**Goal:** a Printify product that is correctly built, honestly
described, and priced with the FULL fee stack — so a sale is profit,
not a surprise.

## Build order in Printify

1. **Blueprint** — pick the product (e.g. a unisex jersey tee).
   Choose for the niche: tees are the volume default; sweatshirts and
   hoodies carry bigger tickets and better cold-month demand.
2. **Provider** — your Stage 2 primary for that blueprint. Confirm:
   base cost, shipping cost (CA + US), production time, print area.
3. **Design placement** — upload the Stage 4 PNG, place it inside the
   print area, check placement on the largest and smallest sizes.
4. **Variants** — colours and sizes. Offer the colours the design
   actually works on (Stage 4 check), sizes S–2XL minimum for adult
   apparel (wider if the provider's upcharges are priced in — see
   below).
5. **Mockups** — generate and LOOK at them: front placement, scale,
   colour accuracy. The mockup is what the buyer sees; a sloppy
   mockup is a sloppy listing even if the print is fine.

## Pricing — the formula

```
Profit = (Price + Shipping charged)
       − Printify product cost
       − Printify shipping cost
       − Etsy listing fee        (US$0.20 per listing/4 months)
       − Etsy transaction fee    (6.5% of price + shipping charged)
       − Etsy regulatory fee     (0.50% in Canada, same base)
       − Etsy processing fee     (3% + C$0.25 CA/US buyers; 4% + C$0.25 other intl)
       [− Etsy Offsite Ads fee]  (15%/12% — only on ad-attributed orders)
```

Run it, don't eyeball it:

```bash
python3 -m pipeline.pricing config/pricing.example.json
# or solve backwards — the price that leaves a target profit:
# (set "target_profit" in the JSON and the tool prints the price)
```

Rules that keep the math honest:

- **Size upcharges are real.** Providers charge more for 2XL+. Either
  price the listing at the highest variant's cost, or set variant
  prices — a flat price priced on size M loses money on every 2XL.
- **"Free shipping" is a pricing decision, not a gift.** Etsy search
  rewards it, buyers love it — and the shipping cost moves INTO your
  price, where the percentage fees also bite. The calculator shows
  both ways; `test_free_shipping_bakes_cost_into_margin` proves the
  profit is the same at the same buyer total.
- **Price for the Offsite Ads case before it happens.** If a 15%
  attributed-order fee turns your profit negative, either price with
  headroom or opt out of Offsite Ads while your shop is under the
  US$10,000 threshold (you can, now — after the threshold you can't).
- **Currency discipline.** Price, costs, and fees in ONE currency in
  the calculator. Etsy's listing fee is USD; convert it once
  (US$0.20 ≈ C$0.28 at writing — check the day's rate) and move on.

## Stage checklist

- [ ] Blueprint + provider confirmed; real costs pulled from the
      live catalog and recorded (not example numbers)
- [ ] Design placed and checked on smallest/largest sizes
- [ ] Variants limited to colours the design passes on
- [ ] Size upcharges priced in
- [ ] Calculator run: profit > 0 at list price, AND survivable
      (or a deliberate opt-out decision) under a 15% Offsite Ads sale
- [ ] Mockups eyeballed — they are the listing photos
- [ ] Product saved as DRAFT in Printify — publishing is Stage 7

## Shop-type note

Printify construction is the `pod-apparel` path; `pet-accessories`
builds the same way (pet blanks instead of shirts — bandanas, beds,
mats — with the partner's real costs, and sets/bundles priced as
the margin play). For the **digital presets** (`digital-downloads`,
`digital-templates`, `stl-files`, `coloring-books`) there is
nothing to build in Printify: the "build" is assembling the
deliverable files (≤5 per listing, ≤20 MB each — for templates, the
delivery PDF carrying the files or the Canva template link) and
this stage reduces to **pricing** — run the calculator with
`product_cost: 0, shipping_cost: 0, shipping_charged: 0` (the
presets' pricing configs already do), so what you see is the pure
fee stack on your price. For physical prints of your own STL
models, cost the unit honestly (filament + power + wear + 5–15%
failures + time) and price at 3–5× — see
`docs/research-findings-2026-11-23.md` §C.
