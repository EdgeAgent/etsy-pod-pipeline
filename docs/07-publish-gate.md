# Stage 7 — The Publish Gate

**Goal:** nothing goes live by accident, by momentum, or by an
agent's enthusiasm. Every product crosses this gate one at a time.

## The doctrine (Kevin's rule, stated plainly)

> **Nothing sends, posts, or publishes without the human's explicit
> approval of that exact action.**

Applied here: the human approves **the exact product, with the exact
design, at the exact price, with the exact listing pack** — and only
then does it publish. Approval of one product is not approval of the
next. Approval last week is not approval today. A draft sitting in
Printify is not a soft yes.

## What the human is shown before deciding

One screen/page per product, containing:

- [ ] The design at full size (Stage 4 QC already passed)
- [ ] The mockups the buyer will see
- [ ] The variants and their prices (incl. size upcharges)
- [ ] The pricing breakdown from `pipeline/pricing.py` — profit at
      list price, and the Offsite Ads case
- [ ] The complete listing pack (Stage 6): title, 13 tags, materials,
      description, FAQ
- [ ] The IP checklist result for this design and this text

## The publish sequence (after approval)

1. In Printify, publish the approved product to the Etsy shop —
   Printify creates the listing with your title, description, tags,
   mockups, and prices.
2. **Read it back on Etsy.** Open the live listing as a buyer would:
   title intact, tags present, prices per variant correct, mockups
   the right ones, shipping profile sane. Publishing tools truncate
   and mangle fields; the read-back is how you know what actually
   shipped. (Same discipline as the YouTube pipeline's Studio
   read-back: never claim live from the publish screen alone.)
3. Record it: listing URL, date, price, pack version — in your shop
   log (a simple file or spreadsheet; Stage 8's weekly review uses
   it).
4. Turn on automatic order fulfilment in Printify (Stage 2) once the
   first listing is verified live.

## If anything differs from what was approved

Stop. Fix the draft, re-show the human the changed item, get a fresh
yes. A "small change" to price or design after approval is a new
approval — that is the entire point of the gate.

## Batch publishing

Approving a batch means the human saw every item in the batch, one
line each, with its price and profit. "Publish the rest like this
one" is acceptable ONLY if the human said it about items they were
shown. The agent never extends a yes by analogy.

## Stage checklist

- [ ] Approval packet assembled (design, mockups, variants, pricing,
      pack, IP result)
- [ ] Human approved THIS product + THIS pack, explicitly
- [ ] Published via Printify → read back live on Etsy, field by field
- [ ] Listing URL + date + price logged
- [ ] Auto-fulfilment enabled only after first verified listing

## Shop-type note

The gate is identical for every shop type — the human approves the
exact item + pack — but the read-back differs: for the **digital
presets**, publish as a **Digital** listing, then read it back
*and test-download your own files* (open the ZIP, print the STL,
cut the SVG, follow the template link, print the coloring pages)
before calling it live. A file the buyer can't use is a defect
found late. For **pet accessories**, the read-back includes the
personalisation fields and the production-partner disclosure —
both must appear on the live listing exactly as approved.
