# The mixed model — your products + affiliate picks

Every preset in this repo runs **two revenue lanes**:

1. **Your own products**, sold on Etsy, through the eight stages.
   This is the business: your designs, your margin, your reviews.
2. **Affiliate picks** — proven, already-selling third-party
   products in the same niche, promoted **off Etsy** for commission.
   Each preset ships a starter list (`affiliate_picks`): real
   product *categories* with researched price bands — never invented
   ASINs or specific product claims. You pick the actual products,
   on the program's site, when you write the content.

The lanes never mix inside a listing. This doc is where the rules
live; they were researched from primary sources on 2026-11-23
(sources at the bottom, and in the findings addendum §H).

## Why affiliate links can never go in an Etsy listing

There is no Etsy sentence that says "affiliate links are banned" —
the ban is functional, and it is total in practice:

- Etsy's **Seller Policy** (§2(a)(7), updated 2026-10-05) forbids
  uploading content — listings, messages, photos, text — that
  *"encourages or facilitates an off-platform transaction."*
- The **Off-Platform Transactions Policy** (2026-10-05) spells it
  out: no instructing a member to purchase through *"another online
  marketplace"*, no off-platform discounts or incentives, **no QR
  codes** directing members off Etsy.
- A listing must offer an item for sale; a listing created *"for the
  purpose of sharing a referral code"* is itself prohibited (§1(b)).
- **Etsy Messages** are doubly closed: the Seller Policy bars
  unsolicited promotions and off-platform directing in messages, and
  Etsy's own Affiliates Policy names Etsy Convos as a channel where
  affiliate links may never be posted.

So: **no affiliate links in listings, descriptions, photos, FAQs,
shop announcements, shop sections, or messages.** A shop
announcement used to steer buyers elsewhere fails the same test as
a listing. The one designated links area — your shop's About/links
field — is for *identity* links (your website, your socials), not
for steering a purchase of the same item elsewhere. Sharing where
you are ≠ directing a purchase off Etsy.

## Where affiliate picks DO get used

On channels you own, declared to the program, with disclosure:

- **Your blog / website** — gift guides, reviews, tutorials ("the
  markers my coloring-book buyers ask about", "the planner setup I
  recommend"). The canonical affiliate home.
- **Public social posts** on your declared accounts — public
  Facebook Page or open group (not a personal profile, not a closed
  group), Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Twitch, X.
- **A link-in-bio page you control** — cleanest when it routes to
  your own post carrying the links; the click path must never hide
  that the destination is Amazon.
- **Opt-in email** — allowed for Amazon under the current (April
  2026) Associates policies *only* to subscribers who opted in, and
  expressly allowed for Etsy's own affiliate program. The widely
  repeated "Amazon bans all email links" claim is outdated; the
  safe pattern most sellers use is still email → your post → links.
- **Your Amazon Storefront** (Influencer Program), shared from your
  declared social profiles.

## The Amazon Associates rules that actually bite

Researched from the Operating Agreement (updated 2025-10-15) and
Program Policies (updated 2026-04-14):

- **Disclosure, verbatim-ish, everywhere:** *"As an Amazon Associate
  I earn from qualifying purchases"* — stated clearly and
  prominently on your site and anywhere links appear (Agreement §5).
  The FTC's rule sits underneath: the material connection must be
  clear and conspicuous, in the post itself, not behind a "more".
- **The cookie is 24 hours — not 180 days.** A session ends 24
  hours after the click, or at order, or at another associate's
  click. The exception: items **added to the cart** in that session
  still pay if the order completes within **89 days** of the click.
  (180 days is two other things: the new-account review window —
  3 qualifying sales in 180 days — and the ship-and-pay condition.
  Blog posts quoting a "180-day cookie" are wrong.)
- **Never buy through your own links** — not for yourself, not for
  family, friends, employees or contractors, not for resale (§6(u)).
- **No offline promotion:** no Special Links in printed material,
  ebooks, mailings, or oral solicitation (§4). A PDF download is
  offline material in substance — keep links out of your Etsy
  digital files too, for both programs' sake.
- **No cloaking:** no link shorteners or buttons that make it
  unclear the destination is Amazon (§6(v)/(w)); use Amazon's own
  tagged links as provided. Don't show prices unless Amazon serves
  them in the link or via its API.
- **Rates are category-based and change** (roughly 1–5% on the
  categories these presets touch; e.g. Pets ~3%, "All Other" 4% on
  Amazon.com — the .ca table differs). Verify the current table in
  your own Associates Central before quoting a rate anywhere.

## The Etsy Affiliates lane (bonus, different program)

Etsy runs its own affiliate program (via Rakuten, per the live
policy page): affiliate links to Etsy listings — including,
unusually, **your own shop's**, which Etsy expressly permits with
ownership declared — on approved channels only (your public socials,
blog, website, subscriber email; never Etsy Convos or closed
groups). Cookie: **7 days**, last click. Commission is set case by
case in the policy text. It's a legitimate second lane for the same
content: a gift guide can carry your Etsy listing *and* Amazon
picks, each under its own program's rules.

## The honest limits of lane 2

- Affiliate income is rented land: rates, cookies, and programs
  change unilaterally (Amazon has cut category rates repeatedly).
  Lane 1 — your own products and reviews — is the asset.
- Promote only products you can describe honestly. A pick you
  haven't used gets framed as what it is ("the one buyers in this
  niche rate highest"), never as a personal testimonial.
- Disclosure is not fine print. Undisclosed affiliate promotion is
  an FTC problem and an Amazon termination reason, not a style
  choice.
