# Stage 8 — Orders & Care

**Goal:** once listings are live, the machines do the fulfilment and
the human does the part machines can't: trust.

## What happens automatically

1. Buyer pays on Etsy → money lands in your Etsy Payment account
   (minus the per-sale fees, Stage 5).
2. The order appears in Printify (connected channel, Stage 2).
3. With auto-fulfilment on, Printify charges your payment method,
   produces the item at the chosen provider, and ships it to the
   buyer with tracking — which syncs back to Etsy.
4. Etsy deposits your balance to your bank on your chosen deposit
   schedule (daily/weekly/biweekly/monthly options in Etsy Payments).

You touch none of that. Your job is watching the edges:

## What the human still watches

- **Printify balance/costs** — production charges are per order and
  immediate; a card failure pauses fulfilment. Check the card and
  Printify account health weekly, minimum.
- **Buyer messages** — answer within 24 hours (Etsy's Star Seller
  standard; response speed is a real ranking input). Most messages
  are sizing and "where is my order" — the Stage 6 FAQ prevents
  half of them; tracking answers most of the rest.
- **Problem orders** — damaged/misprinted/wrong items: apologize,
  get a photo, and use Printify's reprint/refund flow; your listing
  promised "we make it right", so make it right fast. A handled
  problem often reviews better than a smooth order.
- **Reviews** — read every one. A complaint about print quality,
  sizing, or shipping time is a Stage 4/5/6 defect report, not just
  a bad day. Patterns (3+ similar complaints) trigger a fix at the
  source stage, not another apology.
- **Payouts & fees** — reconcile monthly: Etsy deposits vs. the fee
  stack you priced with. If real fees drift from the calculator's
  rates, Etsy changed something — re-verify
  (`fees-verified-2026-11-23.md` is a dated snapshot, not scripture)
  and re-price.
- **Listing renewals** — listings expire every 4 months (US$0.20 to
  renew). Auto-renew is on by default; decide per listing at review
  time whether a dead listing deserves another 4 months.

## The weekly review loop (30 minutes, same time each week)

1. **Numbers**: orders, revenue, Printify costs, ad-attributed share.
   Profit per design, not per shop — designs are the unit that lives
   or dies.
2. **Winners**: any design selling 3+ in a week gets a Stage 3 pass —
   what adjacent original designs serve the same buyers?
3. **Losers**: listings with traffic and no sales get one fix
   (mockup, title, or price — one variable) or get retired at
   renewal. Listings with no traffic get an SEO pass (Stage 6) once,
   then retirement if nothing changes.
4. **Care log**: message response times, problem orders, review
   themes. Anything recurring goes back to its source stage.
5. **One experiment** for next week: a new design, a price test, a
   new product type. One. Written down, with the result checked at
   the next review.

## Kill criteria (decide before you need them)

- A design that loses money after the real fee stack: reprice once,
  then retire.
- A provider with repeated quality complaints: switch providers
  (Stage 2) and re-run Stage 5 pricing.
- The shop itself: if 90 days of weekly reviews show no traction
  after real iteration, the honest move is to stop paying listing
  renewals and say so — the pipeline's job includes telling you when
  the answer is no (see the README's honest limits).

## Stage checklist

- [ ] Auto-fulfilment ON and verified with the first real order
- [ ] Printify payment method healthy
- [ ] Message response habit: <24h
- [ ] Problem-order flow understood (photo → Printify reprint/refund)
- [ ] Monthly fee reconciliation against the calculator
- [ ] Weekly review on the calendar, with kill criteria written down

## Shop-type note

Printify fulfilment is the apparel path; **pet accessories** run
the same watch, plus personalisation errors (names are the defect
surface) and partner quality drift. The **digital presets** have
no fulfilment to watch — Etsy delivers the files — so this stage's
watch list becomes: download/access problems (answer fast, re-send
help, fix broken files), licence questions and misuse reports,
file-defect reviews ("won't slice", "won't cut", "the link won't
open", "pages print grey" = your Stage 4 QC escaping), dated
planners expiring (refresh annually or unlist), and the same
monthly fee reconciliation and weekly review, unchanged. The
affiliate lane (docs/affiliate-model.md) gets one line in the
weekly review: what content went out, with disclosure, and what it
earned — never inside Etsy, always beside it.
